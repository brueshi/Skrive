# The Slow Read

Reading carefully is the most useful thing I have learned to do. Not reading more books, not reading faster, just reading one sentence at a time and letting it mean what it says.

The first time I noticed this, I was halfway through a history book and realized I had retained almost nothing. The pages had moved; my attention had not. I went back to the start and read at a third of my usual pace.

Something changed. The sentences had weight. I could feel the writer deciding where to put each word. When a chapter ended, I remembered the argument — not because I had studied it, but because I had let it land.

I don't read this way all the time. Some books are furniture; you walk through them. But when the work is worth it, the slow read is the only read that counts.

The trick, if there is one, is patience. The words are not going anywhere.
